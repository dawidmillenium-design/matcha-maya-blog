Field Interview: From Cartagena’s Coastline to Rio’s Carioca Rhythm

ML
By Maya Lin — Lead Remote Work Researcher
Published July 30, 2026 | Updated July 30, 2026

Editorial Note: Our Matcha Maya team personally joined Mateo Silva in Getsemaní to test local connectivity, mobile hotspot performance, and coworking options across Cartagena before his move to Brazil.

Part 1: The Return from Playa Blanca & Cartagena Realities

Matcha Maya (Maya Lin): Teo, welcome back to the mainland! You just spent a week attempting to work remotely out on Playa Blanca in Isla Barú. When our team tested the cell reception out there, we noticed severe drops. Can a creator actually get work done on Playa Blanca, or is it purely a place to unplug? (Check our full 220 Creator Interviews Hub for more coastal workplace breakdowns).

Mateo Silva: Thanks for having me back! Honestly, Playa Blanca is a double-edged sword. When I was out there filming, my jaw dropped because the turquoise water looks incredible. But as someone running a remote business? It will test your sanity.

If you need to hop on daily Zoom calls or upload large 4K video files, Playa Blanca is going to stretch your patience. Most cabañas rely on satellite internet or local hotspots. On a good morning, I pulled 15 to 20 Mbps download speeds on a local SIM. But once 11:00 AM hits, upload speeds regularly drop under 3 Mbps.

Part 2: Lessons Learned in Cartagena’s Digital Nomad Scene

Matcha Maya: You’ve been based in Cartagena for over two years. What are the biggest lessons you've learned about neighborhoods, Wi-Fi, and daily costs?

Mateo Silva: Cartagena is fantastic, but neighborhood selection is critical:

  • Neighborhood Infrastructure: Modern areas like Bocagrande offer high-rise apartments with fast fiber-optic internet (200 to 300 Mbps) and backup power generators. If you want historical charm in Getsemaní, thick colonial stone walls can turn Wi-Fi signals into a crawl. Cross-reference living costs on Numbeo's Cost of Living Index.
  • Co-working Ecosystem: Browse our dedicated City Comparisons Hub to see how Cartagena ranks against other South American hubs.

Part 3: The Pivot — Why Rio de Janeiro, Brazil?

Matcha Maya: What made you choose Rio de Janeiro for your next studio location?

Mateo Silva: It came down to scale, community, and lifestyle balance. Rio de Janeiro offers that rare mix of a massive global city wrapped in stunning natural landscapes.

  1. Digital Nomad Visa: Review official requirements on the Brazil Digital Nomad Visa Portal (VITEM XIV).
  2. Connectivity: Speeds frequently exceed 300 to 500 Mbps in Zona Sul. Verify regional data via the Speedtest Global Index. For audio setup blueprints, see our Podcast Production Proposals.

Part 4: Practical Advice for Global Remote Workers

Mateo Silva: Treat your destination like an operational review. Always verify dedicated fiber lines, carry a 65W portable power bank, and engage with local creator groups before landing. Read more in About Our Remote Work Mission.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can you work remotely from Playa Blanca in Isla Barú?
A: Yes, but speeds drop significantly during peak tourist hours (11 AM - 4 PM), averaging under 3 Mbps upload. A backup power bank and eSIM are mandatory.

Q: What is the average internet speed in Bocagrande, Cartagena?
A: Modern high-rise residential buildings in Bocagrande offer dedicated fiber-optic lines ranging between 100 Mbps and 300 Mbps.
