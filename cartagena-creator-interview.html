Field Interview: From Cartagena’s Coastline to Rio’s Carioca Rhythm

Host: Matcha Maya Remote Work & Travel Network
Guest: Mateo "Teo" Silva (@TeoTravels), Digital Creator & Local Influencer
Location: Getsemaní, Cartagena, Colombia
Topic: Remote Work Logistics, Isla Barú Realities, and Relocating to Rio de Janeiro

Part 1: The Return from Playa Blanca & Cartagena Realities

Matcha Maya: Teo, welcome back to the mainland! You just spent a week attempting to work remotely out on Playa Blanca in Isla Barú, right off the coast of Cartagena. Before we dive into your exciting news about moving down to Brazil, give us the real story. Can a creator actually get work done on Playa Blanca, or is it purely a place to unplug? (Be sure to check our full 220 Creator Interviews Hub for more coastal workplace breakdowns).

Mateo Silva: Thanks for having me back! Honestly, Playa Blanca is a double-edged sword. As a travel creator, your jaw drops because the turquoise water and white sand look incredible on video. But as someone trying to run a business? It will test your sanity.

If you need to hop on daily Zoom calls or upload large 4K video files, Playa Blanca is going to stretch your patience. Most beachfront cabañas rely on satellite internet or local mobile hotspots. On a good morning before the day-trippers arrive, you might snag 15 to 20 Mbps download speeds on a local SIM card. But once 11:00 AM hits and crowds arrive from the ports, the network gets completely jammed. Upload speeds regularly drop under 3 Mbps.

Power reliability is another wild card. Most eco-lodges run generators that shut off between 6:00 AM and 5:00 PM. If you aren't carrying heavy-duty power banks, your workday ends early. That trip actually made something clear to me: I love the Caribbean coast, but my creation workflow needs reliable fiber optics, great coffee shops, and real city infrastructure.

Part 2: Lessons Learned in Cartagena’s Digital Nomad Scene

Matcha Maya: You’ve been based in Cartagena for over two years now. For remote workers looking at Cartagena as a potential base, what are the biggest lessons you've learned about neighborhoods, Wi-Fi, and daily costs?

Mateo Silva: Cartagena is a fantastic, energetic city, but you have to pick your neighborhood based on what your work demands.

  • Neighborhood Infrastructure: Modern high-rise areas like Bocagrande offer great apartments with fast, reliable fiber-optic internet (often 200 to 300 Mbps symmetrical lines) and full power generators, which are lifesavers during coastal storms. If you want historical charm, Getsemaní or the Walled City are gorgeous, but those thick colonial stone walls can turn Wi-Fi signals into a crawl. You can cross-reference living costs with crowd-sourced benchmarks on Numbeo's Cost of Living Index to plan your budget accurately.
  • Cost Metrics: A modern one-bedroom apartment in Bocagrande with ocean views usually costs between and ,200 USD per month. Eating out in Getsemaní averages to USD a meal, though local market lunches in Bazurto can be as cheap as USD.
  • Co-working Ecosystem: Spotting a reliable workspace is key. You can browse our dedicated City Comparisons Hub to see how Cartagena ranks against other South American hubs for desk prices and internet consistency.

Cartagena is amazing for a few months, but as my production needs grew—especially for podcast recording and heavy video editing—I started craving a larger metropolis with a broader tech scene.

Part 3: The Pivot — Why Rio de Janeiro, Brazil?

Matcha Maya: That leads right into your big news: you’re packing up your setup and relocating to Rio de Janeiro! What made you choose Rio, and how are you planning the move?

Mateo Silva: It came down to scale, community, and lifestyle balance. Cartagena gave me my start, but Rio de Janeiro offers that rare mix of a massive global city wrapped in stunning natural landscapes.

When you evaluate Rio as a remote work base, a few major perks jump out:

  1. Digital Nomad Visa: Brazil offers a clear path for location-independent workers. You can review official entry requirements on the Brazil Digital Nomad Visa Portal (VITEM XIV) to see minimum income rules and application steps. The administrative clarity makes long-term planning much simpler.
  2. Connectivity & Co-working: Neighborhoods like Copacabana, Ipanema, and Botafogo have exceptional fiber-optic coverage, frequently exceeding 300 to 500 Mbps. Before picking a neighborhood, I always check the Speedtest Global Index to verify regional internet infrastructure. Plus, Rio has a mature co-working network with spaces offering dedicated podcast studios and soundproof booths. For a deeper look into audio setups, check out our Podcast Production Proposals.
  3. Creative Synergy: Rio is a major media engine. The opportunity to collaborate with local audio engineers, video producers, and fellow creators is huge compared to smaller coastal towns.

Part 4: Practical Advice for Global Remote Workers

Matcha Maya: For creators reading this who want to balance living in beautiful places with keeping their productivity high, what is your top piece of advice?

Mateo Silva: Treat your choice of destination like an operational review rather than a holiday search. Here is my personal checklist before booking a long-term stay:

  • Hardware & Power Backup: Always double-check if your apartment has dedicated fiber-optic lines routed straight to the unit, rather than a weak shared building router. Always carry a portable 65W power bank that can keep your laptop running for at least four hours during outages.
  • Secondary Network Backups: Never rely on just one connection. The second I land in a new country, I get a local physical SIM card or eSIM with unlimited data to use as a mobile hotspot if primary Wi-Fi drops out.
  • Local Community Engagement: Tap into local creator groups before you land. Reaching out to local expats or nomad groups will help you navigate housing, safety, and neighborhood choices way faster than reading outdated blogs.
